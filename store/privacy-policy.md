# World Mapper privacy policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

World Mapper is a measurement experiment. It is built to collect as little about you as it can while still being able to compare readings between people.

## What is saved when you take a reading

* The angle you measured: how high the Sun or Moon stood above level, in degrees.
* How accurate that reading is likely to be.
* The date and time of the reading.
* Which object you pointed at, the Sun or the Moon.
* Your location, rounded to roughly one kilometre (two decimal places of latitude and longitude).
* Your phone's type and browser, for example "iPhone, iOS 18". This is used to spot problems affecting one kind of phone.
* Whether the level check was done, whether the phone was upside down, and whether you aimed by hand.
* A random code for your phone, such as "p-4f2a9c", created on your device so several readings from the same phone can be grouped together.

## What is never saved

* Your name, email address, or any account. There is no sign-in.
* Any photograph. The camera is used to aim on screen, and the image is not stored or sent anywhere.
* Your exact location.
* Any advertising identifier, and any identifier that follows you to other apps or websites.

## Why

Readings are only useful next to other people's readings. The time and the rounded location are what make two readings comparable; everything else is there to judge how reliable a reading is.

## Where it is stored and who sees it

Readings are sent to World Mapper's own storage, hosted on Wix (Wix.com Ltd). They are not sold, and they are not shared with advertisers or data brokers. Summaries and maps built from the readings may be published, and those show patterns across many readings, never a person.

## Children

The app has no sign-in and collects no personal details, so it can be used by anyone. Readings are taken outdoors, and the usual advice applies: never look straight at the Sun.
